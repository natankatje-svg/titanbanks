# Ad 06 — Anker Prime Power Bank 26K 300W: "Dual-Port Fast Recharging — Ready in Minutes"

Source: `raw_ads/media-f7b6c1f2e000.jpg`
Format: 1:1 square (Meta feed)
Brand: Anker (Prime sub-line)
Product: Anker Prime Power Bank 26K 300W — **direct category competitor**

## 1. Visual hierarchy
1. ANKER logo + "Anker Prime" search-bar (top)
2. **"Dual-Port Fast Recharging / Ready in Minutes"** (large white headline — focal)
3. "Anker Prime Power Bank (26K, 300W)" (product name)
4. **"250W Max"** (mid-frame blue gradient stat, secondary focal)
5. Product hero on a wood desk, plugged into two wall outlets via braided cables, blue display lit
6. Hint of a backpack right edge (lifestyle context cue)

## 2. Layout structure
Real-environment composition: warm wall, wooden desk, plug sockets, braided cables, partial backpack. Headline top, product mid-right, supporting stat overlapping product. Diagonal cable energy from upper-left wall plugs down into product.

## 3. Primary focal point
The benefit headline "Ready in Minutes." The 250W stat is the proof. Product is the actor.

## 4. Secondary elements
Two wall chargers (proof of "dual-port"), braided premium cables (signal craft), blue display readout (alive/working), backpack edge (use-case hint: travel/work).

## 5. Visual flow
Headline → product name → eye drops to "250W Max" stat → traces cables back up to wall plugs → understands the dual-port story → notices backpack → contextualizes use.

## 6. Design elements
- Real-environment lighting (not studio black)
- Warm peachy wall + cool product = temperature contrast
- Braided cables = craft signal
- Blue gradient on the spec stat
- Same Anker typography system

## 7. Copy breakdown
- Brand: ANKER · Anker Prime
- Headline: Dual-Port Fast Recharging / Ready in Minutes
- Sub: Anker Prime Power Bank (26K, 300W)
- Spec stat: 250W Max

## 8. Psychological angle
Benefit-led, not feature-led. "Ready in Minutes" sells the *outcome* (no waiting), not the spec. The 250W proves the claim is real.

## 9. Why it works
- Headline answers "so what?" before viewer asks
- In-environment shot makes the product feel real, owned, in use
- Cables narratively connect wall → product (visual storytelling)
- Backpack edge tells the audience who this is for without showing a person

## 10. What to preserve for Titan X
- **Benefit headline → spec proof** structure (e.g. "Built for the 10% moment / 50,000 mAh")
- **In-environment composition** with quiet lifestyle cues at edges (festival wristband, backpack strap, car keys)
- **Visual storytelling via objects** — cables here, carry-loop or coffee cup for Titan X
- **Single edge-of-frame human-presence cue** without showing a person (per caption library: no body parts)
- **Display alive/lit** in the product shot

## 11. What to adapt / reject for Titan X
- Reject the dual-wall-charger plug story — Titan X is about *not needing* an outlet (opposite narrative). Substitute: plug visible but unused, Titan X foreground = "you don't need that anymore."
- Reject blue spec gradient → solid white type, orange accent only
- Reject Anker search-bar brand lockup → quiet TITANBANKS wordmark only
- Reject the warm-wall environment → matte black or low-key matte grey environment per brand aesthetic
- Reject any wattage claim until supplier confirms
- Keep the in-environment realism but recolor to brand-correct dark cinematic palette
